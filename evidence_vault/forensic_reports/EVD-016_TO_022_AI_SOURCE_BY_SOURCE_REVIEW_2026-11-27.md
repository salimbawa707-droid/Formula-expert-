## EVD-016 — Blagojević et al. (2016), anionic dishwashing liquids

**Identity:** Performance and Efficiency of Anionic Dishwashing Liquids with Amphoteric and Nonionic Surfactants, Journal of Surfactants and Detergents 19(2), 363–372, DOI 10.1007/s11743-015-1784-5. Publisher: https://aocs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1007/s11743-015-1784-5

### Scientific review

The publisher abstract confirms manual-dishwashing comparisons of SLES/AOS with amphoteric CAB and nonionics including cocamide DEA, C12–C15 7EO, C10 7EO, C9–C11 7EO and lauramine oxide. Reported outcomes include CMC, surface tension, cleaning, foaming, biodegradability and irritancy at 17, 23 and 42 °C. The abstract reports improved CMC/surface tension, cleaning and foaming for selected mixtures relative to the binary SLES/AOS reference, with the C9–C11 7EO/AO mixture best among those examined.

The existing vault record specifies six mixture systems, 10 wt.% total surfactant, CMC at 23 °C, a 0.4% free-flow foam test at 42 °C, and plate-count testing at 4 g/L in 16 ± 1 °dH water at 17 and 42 °C. Five plate-count experiments per formulation are reported as means. Raw replicate observations are not present in the reviewed record.

**Use boundary:** manual dishwashing only. Plotted foam height is not a time-resolved foam decay series or half-life. No transfer to laundry powder/liquid or automatic dishwash. AE chain distribution and EO number are part of the grade identity.

## EVD-017 — REACH Annex XVII Entry 46 / 46a, NPE restrictions

**Primary legal references:**
- Regulation (EC) No 552/2009, Annex XVII Entry 46: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=CELEX:32009R0552
- Commission Regulation (EU) 2016/26, Entry 46a: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32016R0026
- ECHA Entry 46 group: https://echa.europa.eu/substance-information/-/substanceinfo/100.239.180

### Legal/scope review

Entry 46 restricts nonylphenol and nonylphenol ethoxylates (NPE) as substances or mixture constituents at concentrations equal to or greater than 0.1% by weight for specified uses, including domestic cleaning. It applies to the specified NPE group and listed uses—not every nonionic surfactant or every alcohol ethoxylate.

Entry 46a is distinct: it restricts placing on the EU market, after 3 February 2021, of specified washable textile articles containing NPE at 0.01% or more by weight of the textile article or each relevant part. This is not a detergent-mixture concentration threshold. Regulatory exceptions in the text must be retained if the rule is implemented.

ECHA identifies the NPE group. Supplier identity must be checked; generic “nonionic” or “ethoxylate” labels are insufficient to classify a material as NPE.

### Applicability and legal caveats

- EU law only; it does not establish Indian legal status.
- Below-threshold concentration is not a general safety approval or universal permission.
- Other REACH restrictions, CLP duties, product-specific rules and national requirements may apply.
- Do not apply Entry 46a's textile-article threshold to detergent composition.
- India-market products require current Indian legal review; do not silently import EU law or infer that no Indian restriction exists.

### Rights review

The source is official regulatory text. Public availability and legal-text status are recorded, but repository redistribution/reproduction rights were not separately adjudicated. Retain UNKNOWN rather than invent a blanket open license.

- [x] Jurisdiction and NPE identity limits documented.

## EVD-018 — Unilever EP3622047B1, liquid laundry detergent composition

**Identity:** EP3622047A1 / EP3622047B1, Liquid laundry detergent composition, patent family including WO2018206202A1. Public patent record: https://patents.google.com/patent/EP3622047B1/en

### Scientific review

The patent describes an aqueous liquid laundry composition with LAS, alcohol ether sulfate (including SLES 3EO), C12–C15 7EO alcohol ethoxylate and alkyl ether carboxylate (AEC). Its claims specify anionic surfactant and AEC ranges, with up to 4 wt.% ethoxylated alcohol nonionic. The example compares AEC addition in a defined wash protocol and pH 8.4 base composition.

The recorded ΔR460 is the incremental reflectance effect associated with adding AEC relative to its control. It is not absolute detergency caused by the nonionic. Nonionic fraction, anionic ratio and AEC condition are coupled variables; this is not an isolated nonionic dose-response.

Do not infer foam half-life, CMC, viscosity, or universal nonionic performance. Do not count examples as external validation pairs for ENG-003/ENG-005. Preserve the exact stain-monitor, water-hardness, dosage and reflectance protocol.

### Patent legal status versus reuse

Google Patents labels the EP record “Revoked” but explicitly warns its legal-status field is an assumption, not a legal conclusion. EPO guidance identifies the European Patent Register as the route for European file status; national validation status must be checked in relevant national registers. A patent disclosure is not a blanket copyright/data license or freedom-to-operate opinion.

## EVD-019 — Malihi et al. (2011), LAS/alcohol ethoxylate interactions

**Identity:** Evaluation of Physico-Chemical Interactions between Linear Alkylbenzene Sulfonate (LAS) and Alcohol Ethoxylates, Tenside Surfactants Detergents 48(5), 395–399, DOI 10.3139/113.110145. DOI: https://doi.org/10.3139/113.110145

### Scientific review

The source studies binary LAS with C12–C14 alcohol ethoxylates, including 7EO and 2EO grades. The accessible author-uploaded text describes mixed CMC, surface tension, calcium sensitivity/solubility and fabric-soil detergency. It reports that a 12% LAS / 3% AE-7 composition required more than 370 ppm Ca2+ to form insoluble Ca(LAS)2 under the stated test, while the same calcium-solubility benefit was not observed for AE-2. This does not establish universal hardness tolerance.

The study's powder detergency chassis is reported to contain 41% STPP. Its detergency outcome cannot be directly transferred to phosphate-free formulas. Water hardness, builder chemistry, fabric/soil combination and AE grade are material conditions.

The existing vault records an AI cross-check of Tables 1–2 and a Gibbs-adsorption n-factor concern. Pure-LAS surface excess depends on correct ionic stoichiometry and source method. Keep the printed value and recalculated interpretation separate; never silently overwrite a source observation with a theoretical correction. Mixed-system effective-n values require their own method justification.

No raw replicate data or external validation pairs are available. This is contextual evidence for ENG-002 only; it does not prove that the current Clint implementation supports LAS/AE-7EO or validate any engine.

### Rights review

ScienceOpen and ResearchGate metadata identify Carl Hanser copyright (2011); no open commercial reuse grant was verified. Author-uploaded full text is not a redistribution license. Keep table/figure reuse and commercial ingestion blocked pending permission.
